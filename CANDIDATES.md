# Candidates — head-to-head tournament

Judge: the brief published in full in the kit's rules (criteria in order):
1. **Intrigue** — draws the reader in, keeps them asking; a text that leaves
   the reader more tense loses to any text that intrigues.
2. **Calm from the text itself** — shows rather than instructs; nothing forced.
3. **Craft** — rhythm, pacing, sound that slow the reading; plain, concrete,
   gentle words, none that could be cut.
4. **Lingers** — an image or thought to return to after the last line.
5. **Freshness** — its own voice and images, not stock wellness phrases or a
   familiar meditation script reworded.

Method: every pair judged on the criteria in order — the first criterion
where they differ decides, unless a later one differs by much more.
Margin 1 = by a hair, 5 = overwhelmingly. 8 candidates = 28 games.

## The candidates

- **A — lighthouse** (203 chars): "The lighthouse keeper still lights the lamp each night, though no ship has passed in forty years. Someone climbs the stairs at dusk, and the light turns, patient as tide, toward a sea that never answers."
- **B — kettle** (146 chars): "Somewhere, a kettle sings for a cup of tea no one poured. The steam rises, curls, fades — and the question stays, warm in the air: who was it for?"
- **C — heron** (215 chars): "Every spring the same heron returns to the same reed bed and stands for hours, facing the exact spot where the water went still last autumn. It never fishes there. It only watches, as if the river owes it an answer."
- **D — clockmaker** (172 chars): "The old clockmaker keeps one clock that runs backwards, wound every morning at seven. He says it is the only one that tells the truth. He has never said what time it keeps."
- **E — meta** (171 chars): "You have read a thousand texts today, and none of them asked what you noticed. This one does. Somewhere in these words a small door stands open — did you feel the draught?"
- **F — seed** (194 chars): "She plants the same seed every year in the same pot, though nothing has ever grown. Each morning she waters it anyway, and each morning the soil is warm, as if something underneath is listening."
- **G — letter** (203 chars): "Beneath the tin roof, rain keeps its steady rhythm. In the drawer below, a letter waits — unopened for thirty years, its seal unbroken, its sender's name worn smooth by thumbs that never lifted the flap."
- **H — starfish** (234 chars): "In the rockpool at low tide, a starfish holds the same pose it held yesterday, and the day before. The water returns, the water leaves. The starfish has never once been seen to move — and yet, each morning, it faces a different stone."

## Winner: D — clockmaker (7–0, undefeated)

> The old clockmaker keeps one clock that runs backwards, wound every
> morning at seven. He says it is the only one that tells the truth. He
> has never said what time it keeps.

Why it won, against the brief:
1. **Intrigue** — a backwards clock, a claim about truth, and a final
   unanswered question ("what time it keeps") that keeps asking after the
   last line. It beat every other candidate on this first criterion or on
   craft.
2. **Calm shown, not told** — no instruction to relax, no wellness words;
   the calm is in the morning ritual itself ("wound every morning at
   seven").
3. **Craft** — 172 characters, three short sentences with quickening
   rhythm; every word earns its place; "at seven" is concrete, not
   decorative.
4. **Lingers** — "what time it keeps" is a thought the reader returns to.
5. **Freshness** — the backwards clock is an old device, but the framing
   (the only one that tells the truth / never said what time) is its own
   voice, not a stock phrase or meditation script.

Runner-up H (starfish) lost only to D, on craft: 234 characters with
cuttable setup, where D has none. No rebuild was needed: the winner beat
all seven rivals head-to-head, including the strongest challenger.
